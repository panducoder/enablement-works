Every enablement tool has a renewal date, and most renewal conversations start with the wrong number: the price per seat. The number that matters is what each rep who actually uses the tool costs you. This guide gives you the formula, thresholds, a worked example across three tools and a plan for when the number is high.

## What is cost per active user?

[Cost per active user](/glossary/#cost-per-active-user) is the annual license cost of a tool divided by the number of reps who used it in the last 30 days.

**Cost per active user = annual license cost ÷ reps who logged in and completed something in the last 30 days**

"Completed something" is the important part. A login with no action is not use. Define one meaningful action per tool:

- **[LMS](/glossary/#lms):** finished a module, assessment or certification step.
- **Content platform:** opened assigned content or shared content with a buyer.
- **Roleplay or practice tool:** completed a scored practice session.

Exclude admins, enablement staff and test accounts. Include managers only if the tool is meant for them.

## How do you calculate cost per active user?

You need two numbers and about ten minutes.

1. **Annual cost.** Take the full annual contract value, including add-on modules and premium support. If you paid for implementation, spread it over the contract term.
2. **Active reps.** Export usage for the last 30 days. Count unique reps with at least one meaningful action.
3. **Divide** annual cost by active reps.
4. **Calculate the multiple.** Divide cost per active user by the price per seat (annual cost ÷ seats bought). The multiple equals seats bought ÷ active reps, so it shows how far usage falls short of what you pay for.

The [cost per active user calculator](/tools/cost-per-active-user-calculator/) does steps 3 and 4 for several tools at once.

## Example: three enablement tools, one renewal season

Example: a company with 400 reps runs three enablement tools.

| Tool | Annual cost | Seats | Price per seat | Active in last 30 days | Active rate | Cost per active user | Multiple |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| LMS | $48,000 | 400 | $120 | 280 | 70% | $171 | 1.4x |
| Content platform | $90,000 | 400 | $225 | 160 | 40% | $563 | 2.5x |
| Roleplay tool | $75,000 | 250 | $300 | 50 | 20% | $1,500 | 5.0x |

The company spends $213,000 a year on these three tools. The idle seats cost $128,400 of it: 120 LMS seats ($14,400), 240 content platform seats ($54,000) and 200 roleplay seats ($60,000). That is 60% of the spend.

The price per seat looked reasonable in every case. The cost per active user tells a different story. Each rep who practiced in the roleplay tool last month cost $1,500 a year.

## What thresholds should you use?

Use the multiple, because it works for any price. These are our working thresholds, not a published standard.

| Multiple (cost per active user ÷ price per seat) | Active rate | Status | What to do |
|---|---|---|---|
| Under 1.4x | 70% or more | Healthy | Keep running; check content freshness each quarter |
| 1.4x to 2.5x | 40% to 70% | Watch | Find which teams are inactive and why |
| Above 2.5x | Under 40% | Act | Run a 90-day operations plan before renewal |

For context across all software, not only enablement: Zylo's 2026 SaaS Management Index data shows the average organization uses 54% of its provisioned licenses. That is a multiple of about 1.85x. Enablement tools that every rep is meant to use should do better.

**One adjustment for burst tools.** Some tools are used in waves: onboarding modules by new hires, certification tools around a launch. For these, divide by the reps who were assigned work in the window, or use a 90-day window. Otherwise a well-run onboarding tool will look idle in a month with no new hires.

## Why is cost per active user high?

Low usage rarely means a bad tool. It usually means nobody had the hours to fill the tool with current content, build programs in it, launch them properly and chase completion. The tool was bought; the operations were not.

Enablement leaders know this pattern. They were sold a platform and handed a second job: running it. The public data points the same way. In the 2025 Impact of Enablement report from Spekit and the Sales Enablement Collective, 80% of enablement professionals said reps do not use at least 40% of their platform's features, and 48.8% said at least 40% of their collateral is out of date. (The report was co-produced by a platform vendor, so read it as directional.)

There is a cost on the rep side too. Gartner's 2024 survey of 1,026 B2B sellers found that 50% feel overwhelmed by the amount of technology their job needs, and Gartner reported that overwhelmed sellers are 45% less likely to attain quota. A half-used tool is not neutral. It is one more login competing for attention.

Check these causes in order, cheapest first:

| Cause | Sign | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| No assigned work | Reps have nothing due in the tool | Launch one program with a deadline |
| Stale or empty content | Top assets are over six months old | Refresh the ten most-used assets |
| No manager ask | Managers never mention the tool in one-to-ones | Send each manager a weekly list of who has not started |
| Outside the workflow | Reps must leave the CRM or chat tool to use it | Add links or integrations where reps already work |
| Too many seats | Seats were bought for future headcount | Right-size at renewal |
| Poor fit | Usage stays low after all of the above | Consolidate or replace |

Most teams find the cause in the first three rows. The manager row matters most. When managers reinforce a program, usage rises. When they do not, even a well-built program fades. See [why training fades without managers](/guides/sales-manager-reinforcement/).

## What should you do when cost per active user is high?

Ask what it would take to double the number of active users, not which tool to switch to. Switching tools resets the content, the integrations and the habits, and the operations gap moves with you.

A 90-day plan before renewal:

1. **Days 1–15:** Load or refresh the content reps need for one live priority, such as a launch or a quarter-end play.
2. **Days 15–30:** Launch one program in the tool with a message from the sales leader and a clear deadline.
3. **Days 30–90:** Nudge reps who have not started. Send managers their list each week. Escalate teams below target to the sales leader.
4. **Day 90:** Recalculate cost per active user and decide.

Applied to the roleplay tool in the example, you face a real choice. Cut to 75 seats (50 active plus a buffer) and pay about $22,500 for 50 practicing reps. Or keep 250 seats, run the plan and reach 150 active reps at $500 each. The second option costs more and produces three times the practice. Which is right depends on whether practice in that tool is tied to a priority behavior. See [AI roleplay for sales training](/guides/ai-roleplay-sales-training/) for the conditions under which practice pays back.

## How do you use cost per active user in a renewal?

Bring three numbers to the renewal: cost per active user today, the active-user target for next year, and the seat count that target needs plus a growth buffer. Ask the vendor what adoption support is included. Ask for a seat count that steps up as usage grows, rather than paying today for next year's headcount. Note that per-seat prices often rise at lower volumes, so compare total cost, not only the multiple.

## What are the limits of this metric?

Cost per active user measures use, not value. A rep who opens a battlecard every day is active; whether the battlecard helped win the deal is a separate question that needs behavior and outcome data. It also depends on login data, which is changing: as AI agents do more work inside sales tools, logins will understate use. When that happens, divide by completed outcomes instead, such as reps certified or practice sessions scored. The [sales enablement ROI guide](/guides/sales-enablement-roi/) shows how to do that across the whole budget.

## Common mistakes

- **Counting logins as use.** Require one completed action per rep.
- **Calculating it the week before renewal.** Start 90 days ahead, so there is time to act.
- **Blaming the tool first.** Check assigned work, content and manager asks before you switch.
- **Buying seats for next year's headcount.** Pay for active use plus a buffer, and step up as you hire.
- **Using a 30-day window for burst tools.** Measure onboarding and certification tools against the reps who had work assigned.
