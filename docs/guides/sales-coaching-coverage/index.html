Coaching coverage: the sales coaching metric most teams can't answer

CoachingUpdated 6 November 20267 min readBy the Enablement Works editorial team

Short answer

Formula: reps coached ÷ active reps × 100. Our rule of thumb: below 50% is a gap, 70–85% is healthy, and above 90% deserves a quality check.

Ask a CRO one question: in the last 30 days, what share of your reps had at least one documented coaching conversation with their manager, based on a real call or a roleplay? Most cannot answer it. That number is coaching coverage, and it tells you more about whether enablement will stick than any completion rate. This guide defines it, gives the formula and target ranges, and shows the three changes that make it measurable and higher.

What is coaching coverage?

Coaching coverage is the percentage of active reps who had at least one documented, evidence-based coaching conversation with their manager in the last 30 days. Programs teach. Managers make it stick. Coverage is the simplest measure of whether the second part is happening at all.

A conversation counts only if it meets four tests:

  1. One rep. It is a one-to-one, not a team meeting.
  2. Evidence. It uses a recorded call, a live call the manager joined, or a scored roleplay.
  3. One behavior. It names a specific behavior, ideally one from the team's shared scorecard.
  4. A next step. It ends with a drill or a thing to try on the next call, and it is written down.

Pipeline reviews, forecast calls and deal inspections do not count. They matter, but they coach the deal, not the rep's skill. See the glossary entry for coaching coverage.

How do you calculate coaching coverage?

Use this formula:

Coaching coverage = reps with at least one documented coaching conversation in the last 30 days ÷ active reps × 100

"Active reps" means quota-carrying reps who worked for most of the 30 days. Exclude reps on leave. Include new hires from their third week. Use a rolling 30-day window, not a calendar month, so the number does not jump at month end.

Coverage alone measures reach. Three companion metrics tell you about depth and quality:

Metric Formula What it tells you
Coaching coverage Reps coached in 30 days ÷ active reps Reach: is anyone left out?
Coaching depth Coaching conversations ÷ reps coached Frequency for those who are coached
Coaching focus Conversations on the quarter's priority skill ÷ all conversations Alignment with the enablement plan
Follow-through Coached reps re-observed on the same behavior within 30 days ÷ reps coached Whether coaching closes the loop

Start with coverage. Add the other three once the logging habit exists.

What is a good coaching coverage rate?

As our rule of thumb, 70–85% coverage in a 30-day window is healthy for a sales team. Below 50%, many reps get no skill coaching at all. Above 90% is possible but rare, and it should prompt a check that the logged conversations are real. These ranges are our working benchmarks, not published industry figures.

Coverage What it usually means What to do
Below 30% Coaching is left to each manager's habits Introduce the scorecard and the logging step
30–50% A few managers coach; most do not Send the weekly manager note; report by team
50–70% The habit is forming Add the focus and follow-through metrics
70–85% Healthy Shift attention to quality and call evidence
Above 90% Excellent, or inflated Sample ten logged conversations and check the evidence

Why not aim for 100%? Holidays, new managers and wide spans of control make it unrealistic. The time math also matters. A manager with ten reps who holds one 30-minute evidence-based conversation per rep each month, plus 15 minutes of preparation, spends about 7.5 hours a month. That is less than two hours a week. With 15 reps, it is almost three hours a week, and coverage drops first.

Why does coaching coverage matter more than completion rates?

Completion tells you a program was delivered. Coverage tells you whether anyone is helping reps apply it on real deals, and survey research ties structured coaching to better results.

The CSO Insights 2019 Sales Enablement Report, based on more than 900 participants, found that 62.9% of organizations left coaching to managers or followed informal guidelines. Only 37.1% had a formal or dynamic approach. Organizations with dynamic coaching, meaning formal coaching aligned to the enablement services reps received, reported a 55.2% win rate. Where coaching was left to managers, the win rate was 41.8%. The study average was 46.4%.

Korn Ferry reports a similar gap. Its research cites a 28% increase in quota attainment and a 32% increase in win rates from effective sales coaching, and a Korn Ferry poll found that 80% of sales leaders had no formal, or an inconsistent, coaching process. Earlier, Matthew Dixon and Brent Adamson wrote in Harvard Business Review (2011) that research by the Sales Executive Council found no other productivity investment close to coaching for improving rep performance.

The constraint is time and insight, not belief. Gartner's 2025 research on data-driven coaching says sales managers know coaching drives performance but lack either the insights or the time to coach well. Coverage makes that constraint visible.

The limit: these are correlations from surveys. Organizations that coach well may also hire, pay and manage well. Coverage is a leading indicator, not a guarantee.

Why can't most sales teams answer the coverage question?

Most teams cannot report coverage because nobody records coaching consistently. Managers coach when they can, on what they notice, to their own standard. Some write notes in a document, some in the CRM, most nowhere. Without a shared definition and a 60-second logging step, the honest answer to "what is our coverage?" is "we don't know".

The three changes that make coverage measurable and higher

Three changes do most of the work. None of them needs a new tool.

  1. A short scorecard of three behaviors. Pick the three behaviors that matter most this quarter and use them everywhere: in content, in roleplays and in manager coaching. When the rep, the roleplay and the manager grade the same three things, coaching gets faster and logging gets easier. The sales skills scorecard guide shows how to build one.
  2. A weekly note to each manager. Every Monday, send each manager one short note per rep: one strength, one gap and one drill, drawn from roleplay scores and recent calls. The manager starts from a draft instead of a blank page. A draft gets used; a blank page gets postponed.
  3. Coverage reported by team, not by manager. Put coverage on the monthly CRO report as a team number. That makes it a shared goal for the sales leader rather than a ranking of managers. Share manager-level data privately.

To record it, create a coaching activity in your CRM or coaching tool with four fields: rep, evidence link, behavior and next drill. If logging takes more than a minute, managers will stop.

Five coaching complaints and their usual causes

The symptom a team reports is usually two steps away from the cause, and the fix is rarely more content.

Complaint Usual instinct Usual cause Fix
"Reps don't log in to practice." Send reminders, change the tool Their manager never asks about it Manager reads scores weekly and mentions them
"Scores plateau." Make scenarios harder Scenarios do not feel like the rep's world Build the scenario from the rep's own open deal
"We're bad at negotiation." Train everyone One cohort is struggling Score by cohort and coach that cohort
"Great feedback, no change." Better feedback No follow-through on a live deal Manager coaches one real deal per rep using the skill
"They did the simulation once." Blame motivation No frequency rule Weekly practice, scored

Sometimes the product really is the problem. Check the manager first, because it is cheaper.

Example: raising coverage from 38% to 78% in a quarter

Example: a sales team has 120 reps and 12 managers. In the first month of logging, 46 reps have a documented, evidence-based coaching conversation. Coverage is 46 ÷ 120 = 38%. One team is at 10%; another is at 80%.

The team introduces a three-behavior scorecard, a Monday manager note and team-level reporting. By month three, two reps are on leave, so 118 reps are active. 92 have a documented conversation. Coverage is 92 ÷ 118 = 78%. Depth is 1.4 conversations per coached rep, and 70% of conversations are on the quarter's priority skill. The 10% team is now at 55%, and its second-line manager gets a private view of which reps are still uncoached.

Common mistakes

  • Counting pipeline reviews as coaching. They coach the deal, not the behavior.
  • Ranking managers in public. It produces logging, not coaching.
  • Logging without evidence. A note that says "discussed discovery" with no call or roleplay attached is not coaching.
  • Coaching everything at once. A conversation that covers five skills teaches none. Keep it to one behavior from the scorecard.

What are the limits of coaching coverage?

Coverage measures reach, not quality. A manager can hit 100% with weak conversations. That is why follow-through and call evidence matter: they show whether the coached behavior appears on the next calls. Coverage also depends on honest logging, so sample a few conversations each month. For the bigger picture of what managers must do beyond coaching, read why sales training fades without managers.

Frequently asked questions

How often should sales managers coach each rep?

At least one documented, evidence-based coaching conversation per rep every 30 days is the floor. Many teams aim for two a month for reps in their first year. Frequency matters less than whether each conversation uses a real call or roleplay and ends with one drill.

What counts as a coaching conversation?

A one-to-one conversation that uses evidence (a recorded call, a live call the manager joined, or a scored roleplay), names one behavior and ends with a specific next step. Pipeline reviews, forecast calls and team meetings do not count.

How do you track sales coaching in a CRM?

Create a coaching activity type with four fields: rep, evidence link, behavior coached and next drill. Logging takes under a minute. Coverage then becomes a simple report of distinct reps with a coaching activity in the last 30 days.

What is a good coaching coverage rate?

As our rule of thumb, 70–85% of active reps in a 30-day window is healthy. Below 50% means many reps are not coached at all. Above 90% is possible, but check that the logged conversations are real and evidence-based.

Does sales coaching improve win rates?

Survey research points that way. The CSO Insights 2019 Sales Enablement Report found a 55.2% win rate for organizations with a dynamic coaching approach, against 41.8% where coaching was left to managers. That is a correlation, not proof of cause, but it is consistent across years of that study.

Should coaching coverage be reported by manager?

Report it by team first. Ranking managers publicly turns coverage into a compliance number and invites fake logging. Share manager-level data privately with each manager and their leader.
