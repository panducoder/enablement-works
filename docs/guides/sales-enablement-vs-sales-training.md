# Sales enablement vs sales training: what's the difference?

> Sales training builds skills and knowledge through programs such as courses, workshops, roleplays and certifications. Sales enablement is the wider, ongoing discipline that gives sellers and their managers what they need to sell well: training plus content, tools, coaching, onboarding and measurement. Training is one service inside enablement, usually the largest, but rarely enough on its own.

Source: https://enablement.works/guides/sales-enablement-vs-sales-training/ · Updated 2026-11-06 · Enablement Works

## Key takeaways

- Sales training builds skills; sales enablement makes sure those skills, plus the right content and tools, show up in real buyer conversations.
- Training is one service inside sales enablement, and CSO Insights found it was the most common enablement service for four years running.
- Training is an event or a program with an end date; enablement is an ongoing system with a roadmap.
- If reps can pass the roleplay but do not do it on calls, you have an enablement problem, not a training problem.
- Revenue enablement extends the same discipline to every customer-facing role, not only sellers.

Sales enablement and sales training are often used as if they mean the same thing. They do not, and the confusion has a cost: companies buy a training program to fix a problem that training cannot fix, then wonder why nothing changed on calls. This guide gives a plain definition of each, a side-by-side comparison, a way to decide which one you need, and how the two work together.

## What is the difference between sales enablement and sales training?

Sales training builds skills and knowledge. Sales enablement is the wider discipline that makes sure those skills, plus the right content, tools and coaching, show up in real buyer conversations. Training is one service inside enablement.

CSO Insights, in its 2019 Sales Enablement Report, defined sales enablement as a strategic, collaborative discipline that provides consistent, scalable services so that customer-facing professionals and their managers can add value in every customer interaction. The same report described sales training as covering selling skills, sales methodologies, sales processes, sales technology and product training, delivered through e-learning, classroom, online and hybrid formats.

Put simply: training answers "can the rep do it?" Enablement answers "does the rep do it, with the right support, on real deals, and can we prove it?" For a longer definition, see [what is sales enablement](/guides/what-is-sales-enablement/).

## Sales enablement vs sales training: side by side

| Dimension | Sales training | Sales enablement |
|---|---|---|
| Purpose | Build a skill or knowledge | Improve sales results through skills, content, tools and coaching |
| Scope | Courses, workshops, roleplays, certifications | Training plus onboarding, content, talk tracks, battlecards, tools, manager coaching support, measurement |
| Shape over time | Event or program with an end date | Ongoing system with a 12-month roadmap |
| Typical owner | Sales training lead, L&D or an outside provider | Head of enablement, reporting to sales or revenue leadership |
| Audience | Reps, sometimes managers | Reps, managers and other customer-facing roles |
| Main measures | Attendance, completion, quiz and certification scores | Ramp time, behavior on calls, content adoption, stage conversion, win rate |
| Main tools | LMS, roleplay tool, classroom | LMS, content platform, roleplay tool, conversation intelligence, CRM |
| Common failure | Skill learned, then forgotten | Too many services, none finished or measured |

## Is sales training part of sales enablement?

Yes. Sales training is the most common service enablement teams provide. The CSO Insights 2019 report found that training services ranked first among enablement services for four years in a row, followed by sales tools. Onboarding ranked sixth and coaching by the enablement team seventh.

The same report made the key point: training can start a change, but it cannot reach enablement's goals alone. It needs ongoing coaching to become effective. Training-transfer research agrees. In Alan Saks and Monica Belcourt's 2006 study of 150 organizations, training professionals estimated that about 62% of trainees applied training immediately, but only 34% a year later. Activities before and after training were more strongly related to transfer than activities during it. Enablement is the "before and after".

## When do you need sales training, and when do you need sales enablement?

You need training when the gap is a skill or knowledge gap and the rest of the system works. You need enablement when the skill exists but does not show up on calls, or when the gap is content, tools, onboarding or measurement. Use this table to diagnose.

| Situation | What you need | Why |
|---|---|---|
| New product launch; reps do not know it yet | Training, inside an enablement launch plan | Knowledge gap, but reps also need talk tracks and battlecards |
| Reps pass the roleplay but do not do it on calls | Enablement: manager reinforcement and call checks | The skill exists; the transfer is missing |
| Reps cannot find the right content in deals | Enablement: content operations | Not a skill gap |
| New hires take more than seven months to ramp | Enablement: an onboarding system, with training inside it | Ramp depends on territory, tools, plan and manager, not only courses |
| One cohort is weak at one skill | Training: a targeted [skill sprint](/guides/continuous-sales-training/) | Narrow, well-defined skill gap |
| The CRO asks what changed after last year's training | Enablement: measurement | Training reports activity; the CRO needs behavior and outcomes |
| A new sales methodology is being adopted | Both | Training teaches it; enablement embeds it in content, CRM and coaching |

## How do sales enablement and sales training work together?

They work as one loop. Enablement diagnoses the gap and sets the priority. Training builds the skill. Content and tools support it in the flow of work. Managers reinforce it. Measurement checks whether it shows up on calls and in deals, and picks the next gap.

1. **Diagnose:** enablement finds the behavior gap from calls, deals and roleplay scores, by cohort.
2. **Build the skill:** training delivers practice, scored against a shared scorecard.
3. **Support the moment:** talk tracks, battlecards and examples are available where reps work.
4. **Reinforce:** managers coach the skill on live deals and ask about practice.
5. **Measure:** results are reported at four levels: activity, practice, behavior and outcome. See [sales enablement metrics](/guides/sales-enablement-metrics/).

Training without steps 1, 3, 4 and 5 is an event. Enablement without step 2 is a content library.

## Does formal sales enablement improve results?

Survey data suggests it does, when it is formal. In the CSO Insights 2019 report, based on more than 900 participants, organizations with sales enablement reported a 49.0% win rate on forecast deals, against 42.5% for those without. Organizations with a formal enablement approach and a written charter reported 55.1%, against 39.2% for those with a random, project-by-project approach. Only 15.8% of organizations were in the formal-and-charter group. These are correlations, but the pattern held across years of the study.

The discipline is also widening. In 2023, Gartner said it expected 60% of enablement functions to be tasked with enabling all client-facing, revenue-generating roles by 2026, which it calls revenue enablement. Gartner's 2024 Market Guide described revenue enablement platforms as broadened from sales enablement to deliver content, learning and coaching to revenue-generating roles. See the [glossary entry for revenue enablement](/glossary/#revenue-enablement).

## Who should own sales training when you have an enablement team?

Enablement should own the sales training plan and how it is measured, even when others deliver parts of it. Delivery can come from several places, and that is fine:

- **Enablement** designs and runs skill programs, certifications and roleplays tied to the sales process.
- **Product marketing** supplies product knowledge and competitive content, reviewed by enablement for the sales context.
- **L&D** often owns company-wide skills, leadership and compliance programs, and may run sales manager development.
- **Sales leaders and top reps** teach real examples from current deals.
- **Outside providers** bring a methodology or extra production capacity.

The rule is one plan, one scorecard and one owner of the results. When three teams train reps on three different frameworks in the same quarter, reps learn that none of them matters. A written enablement charter, with services, audiences and measures, is the simplest way to settle ownership.

## Example: the same product launch, run two ways

Example: a 200-rep company launches a new product.

| | Training only | Enablement with training inside |
|---|---|---|
| What happens | Two-hour virtual session, recorded | Same session, plus a certification in week one, one roleplay per use case, a talk track and battlecard in the CRM, a manager coaching guide and a call check at day 60 |
| Enablement hours | About 40 | About 140 |
| What you can report at day 60 | 92% attended | 92% attended, 81% certified, new product mentioned on 30% of relevant calls, 45 opportunities created |
| What the CRO learns | The session happened | Whether reps are selling it, and which teams are not |

The extra 100 hours buy the evidence. Without them, the launch report says only that people attended.

## Common mistakes

- **Buying training to fix a transfer problem.** If reps can do it in the roleplay, more training will not change calls.
- **Calling the training team "enablement" without changing its scope.** A new name with the same services and measures changes nothing.
- **Measuring enablement by training completion.** Completion is the first of four levels, not the result.
- **Splitting ownership without a single plan.** When L&D, product marketing and sales each train reps separately, reps get mixed messages. One owner should hold the plan.

## What are the limits of this distinction?

The line between training and enablement blurs in small teams, where one person does both. That is fine, as long as someone owns the full loop from diagnosis to measurement. The survey data cited here shows association, not cause. And enablement cannot fix a weak product, a bad territory plan or a broken compensation plan. ## FAQ

### Is sales training part of sales enablement?

Yes. Sales training is one of the services sales enablement provides, alongside content, tools, onboarding, coaching support and measurement. In most enablement teams it is the largest single service.

### What is the difference between sales enablement and L&D?

Learning and development usually serves the whole company with general skills, leadership and compliance programs. Sales enablement serves customer-facing roles with programs tied to the sales process, buyer and pipeline, and it measures results in sales terms such as ramp time and win rate.

### Do I need sales enablement or sales training?

If you have a specific, well-defined skill gap and managers ready to reinforce it, a training program may be enough. If reps cannot find content, ramp slowly, or pass training but do not change on calls, you need enablement.

### What is revenue enablement?

Revenue enablement applies the sales enablement discipline to every revenue-generating role, such as customer success, presales, partners and marketing, not only sellers. Gartner expected 60% of enablement functions to be tasked with enabling all client-facing, revenue-generating roles by 2026.

### Who should own sales training if there is an enablement team?

Enablement should own the sales training plan and its measurement. Delivery can come from enablement, L&D, product marketing, sales leaders or outside providers, but one owner should decide what gets trained, in what order, and how it is reinforced.

### Can sales training work without sales enablement?

It can teach a skill, but it often fades. Without content that supports the skill, manager reinforcement and a check of real calls, most of what was trained is not used months later.
