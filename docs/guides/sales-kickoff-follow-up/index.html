After the sales kickoff: a 90-day reinforcement plan

Updated 6 Nov 20267 min read4 sourcesEnablement Works editorial team

Key takeaways

  • The kickoff gets the budget, but the 90 days after it decide whether the money pays off.
  • Certify every rep on the core message within 14 days, while the event is still fresh.
  • Build one scored roleplay per new play and give managers the same scorecard reps are scored on.
  • Report to the CRO at days 30, 60 and 90 with activity, practice, behavior on calls and early outcomes.
  • Our rule of thumb: plan as many enablement hours for the 90 days after the kickoff as for building the event.

Every year, sales organizations spend heavily on a kickoff: new messaging, new plays, a big room and a lot of energy. Then the next month starts. The event gets the budget, and the 90 days after it get almost nothing: no practice, no manager follow-up and no check of whether the new message is used on calls. This guide gives you a week-by-week plan for those 90 days, the reports your CRO should see, and a worked example with hours and numbers.

Why does sales kickoff content fade so fast?

Kickoff content fades because people forget quickly after a single exposure, and nothing in the weeks after the event asks reps to use what they heard. Memory research and training-transfer research point the same way.

The forgetting curve is real and has been replicated. In 2015, Jaap Murre and Joeri Dros repeated Hermann Ebbinghaus's 1880s experiment and published it in PLOS ONE. Their results closely matched the original: forgetting is fastest soon after learning, and the rate slows over time. The limit: it was one subject learning nonsense syllables, not a seller learning a talk track. But the shape is the useful part. A message heard once in January is mostly gone by March unless it is used again.

Spacing helps. A 2006 meta-analysis in Psychological Bulletin by Nicholas Cepeda and colleagues reviewed 839 assessments from 317 experiments. It found that spreading study sessions out improves retention, and that the best gap between sessions grows as the time you need to remember grows. For a kickoff, that means practice spread over weeks, not one more session on day two.

Work-environment support matters most. Alan Saks and Monica Belcourt surveyed training professionals in 150 organizations (Human Resource Management, 2006). Their respondents estimated that about 62% of trainees applied training immediately, 44% after six months and 34% after a year. The authors also found that activities before and after training were more strongly related to transfer than activities during it. These are estimates by training staff, not observed behavior, but the direction is clear: the follow-up does more than the event.

Who should own the 90 days after the kickoff?

The head of enablement should own the plan and the reports, front-line managers should own practice and coaching for their reps, and the CRO should sponsor it. Name all three before the kickoff. If the owners are named afterward, the plan usually starts in week four, when most of the forgetting has already happened.

Budget the hours at the same time as the event. Our rule of thumb: plan as many enablement hours for the 90 days after the kickoff as for building the kickoff itself. If your team cannot cover both, the enablement capacity calculator shows the gap in hours before it shows up as a kickoff with no follow-up.

The 90-day kickoff reinforcement plan, week by week

The plan has four parts: a short certification in the first two weeks, one roleplay per new play, weekly nudges with a list for each manager, and a CRO report at days 30, 60 and 90. The table shows when each part runs.

Certification in the first 14 days

Test the situation, not the knowledge. A good kickoff certification asks the rep to deliver the core message to a realistic buyer: a recorded two-minute opening, or a short roleplay where the buyer pushes back once. Score it from 0 to 100 on three behaviors, with a pass line at 70. A quiz about the new messaging framework tells you who can recognize the right answer. It does not tell you who will say it to a CFO. See the glossary entry on certification for the difference.

One roleplay per new play

If the kickoff introduced three plays, build three roleplays, and release one a week. Each roleplay uses the same three-behavior scorecard that managers use when they listen to calls. One shared scorecard is what turns practice into coaching: the rep, the roleplay tool and the manager are all grading the same thing.

Nudges and manager lists

Send reps who have not started a short nudge on days 3, 7 and 10, from their sales leader rather than from "Enablement". Send each manager a list every Monday: who has not started, who failed, who scored highest. If completion is below 80% at day 14, escalate to the second-line manager. Most completion problems are manager problems, and a manager who sees a named list acts faster than one who sees a percentage.

What should the day 30, 60 and 90 reports to the CRO show?

Each report adds one level of evidence. Day 30 shows activity and practice. Day 60 adds behavior on real calls. Day 90 adds early outcomes. This follows the four levels of evidence and fits on one page.

Report Activity Practice Behavior Outcome
Day 30 Certification completion by team Certification pass rate, roleplay attempts per play Not yet Not yet
Day 60 Roleplay completion by team Average score per play, change since day 14 Share of sampled calls where the new message is heard, against baseline Not yet
Day 90 Final completion Retest results for the bottom quartile Call evidence by team, coaching coverage Stage conversion for opportunities where the play was used, against baseline

Report by team, not by rep. A CRO needs to see where adoption is weak, and managers need a shared goal rather than a ranking. The one-page CRO report guide has a template.

Example: a 300-rep kickoff, followed up for 90 days

Example: a company with 300 reps and 30 managers runs a kickoff that introduces three new plays. The kickoff costs $1.2 million, or $4,000 per rep, including travel.

  • Before the event: enablement samples 120 recorded discovery calls. The new problem-first opening is heard on 12% of them. This is the baseline.
  • Day 14: 255 reps have passed certification (85%). Two teams are below 60%, and their second-line manager gets the list.
  • Day 30: 210 reps (70%) have completed at least two of the three roleplays. Average score on the hardest play is 61.
  • Day 60: a new sample of 120 calls shows the opening on 38% of calls, against the 12% baseline. Managers documented coaching on a live deal for 60% of reps.
  • Day 90: the retested bottom quartile moved from an average of 52 to 68. Discovery-to-proposal conversion is reported against last year's same quarter, with the caveat that other things changed too.

The follow-up took about 180 enablement hours: 40 to build the certification, 60 for three roleplays, 36 for nudges and manager lists (3 hours a week for 12 weeks), 24 for three reports and 20 for a manager coaching pack. That is roughly a third of one enablement person's quarter. At a fully loaded rate of $150 an hour, it costs about $27,000, or just over 2% of the event.

Now compare the cost per rep who uses the message. If 30% of reps use the new opening without follow-up, the kickoff cost $13,333 per rep who changed. If the follow-up lifts that to 60%, the cost falls to about $6,667. The hours are small next to the event.

Common mistakes

  • Starting the follow-up after the event. If the certification and roleplays are built in February, the plan starts when most of the forgetting has already happened. Build them before the kickoff.
  • Testing knowledge instead of delivery. A quiz pass rate looks good on a slide and predicts very little about calls.
  • Giving managers a dashboard instead of a list. Managers act on names and one clear ask, not on a new login.
  • Reporting only completion. A 94% completion rate says the program was delivered. It does not say the message reached a single customer.
  • Skipping the baseline. Without a pre-kickoff measure of call behavior, the day 60 number has nothing to beat.

What are the limits of this plan?

The plan shows a believable chain from kickoff to calls, not proof that the kickoff caused a change in win rate. Seasonality, new hires and pricing changes all move outcomes in the same quarter. Behavior evidence also depends on recorded calls. Where calls are not recorded, use roleplay scores, manager observations and CRM stage data, and say so in the report. And the plan needs managers who show up. If managers will not coach for 30 minutes a week, read why sales training fades without managers before you plan the next event, and track coaching coverage from day one.

Frequently asked questions

How long should sales kickoff reinforcement last?

Plan for 90 days. That is long enough for reps to use the new message on real calls and for managers to coach it, and short enough to keep the focus. After day 90, fold the plays into your normal skill sprints and coaching rhythm.

What is a sales kickoff certification?

It is a short, scored test of whether a rep can deliver the core message in a realistic situation, such as a recorded two-minute pitch or a roleplay with a scripted buyer. It is not a quiz. Run it in the first 14 days and set a clear pass line, such as 70 out of 100.

How do you measure whether a sales kickoff worked?

Measure four levels: activity (who completed), practice (who passed certification and roleplays), behavior (is the new message heard on recorded calls) and outcome (did stage conversion or win rate move). Set the baseline before the kickoff, or you will have nothing to compare against.

Who should own the follow-up after a sales kickoff?

The head of enablement owns the plan and the reporting. Front-line managers own their reps' practice and coaching. The CRO sponsors it and reads the day 30, 60 and 90 reports. Name these owners before the kickoff, not after.

What if we do not record sales calls?

Use roleplay scores, certification results, manager observations from ride-alongs or call shadowing, and CRM stage data. The behavior evidence is weaker, but you can still show a believable chain from practice to pipeline.

How much time does a 90-day kickoff follow-up take?

For a team of about 300 reps, our estimate is 150 to 200 enablement hours across the quarter, plus about 30 minutes a week from each manager. Most of the hours go into building certification and roleplays, which can be done before the event.
